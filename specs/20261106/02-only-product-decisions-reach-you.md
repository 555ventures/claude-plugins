# Only product decisions reach you

## Intent
- You asked: "imagine you are programmer, I am a project manager, and I have clients. Whenever there are changes in architecture, behaviour, major UI/UX changes, etc etc. I need to discuss with clients." Less human intervention overall, but real changes must still reach me: v8 was too quiet on niko-hub and shop-bltr.
- I will build:
  - Six stops stay (plan forks, the lock yes and heads-up, `unmet`, `assumption` with an ask arm, the new `unclear`, the reviewer's `needs-decision`); every other stop becomes a rule plus a visible line. Pending kinds shrink to `assumption`, `decision`, `unmet`. One guide with real examples says who decides what.
  - The agent that found the fork writes the question the way a colleague would say it: what is going on, a real example, what was found, what it would do, then the question. No sentence template. One deterministic lint, shared by every stop including plan forks, refuses only jargon, code tokens and overlong text.
  - `/spec:plan` prepares before any question, unasked: quick best-practice research by a cheap agent, then a step-by-step walk through the expected user's flow. Not fully understanding what the user wants is a reason to ask; questions come at most three at a time, in as many rounds as it takes.
  - Fable looks for a better or structural approach before a real product question reaches the user, and the recommendation comes from that. Never for wording, small fixes or internal details.
  - An Intent line `Users will notice:`, a required `userVisible` list in the review, and two report sections, "What users will notice" and "Decided without you", on run and on fanout.
  - A dismissed question is asked once more, explained; a second dismissal pauses that work, queues the question for you, and the rest continues. No answer is ever invented.
  - The `style` plugin's output style matches: decisions and questions in the same voice, the verdict line and the 🎯 block kept.
  - q189: the person who decides is you or the user; `Client sign-off` becomes `User sign-off`, with the reader and `/spec:migrate` changing together; the mocks client becomes the reviewer; the template stops saying JJ.
- I will not touch:
  - Mocks, prototype, release and genesis stops, beyond the q189 wording; the `model:` lines of commands other than plan.
  - HTTP client wording; ADR and past spec bodies, beyond the sign-off line `/spec:migrate` renames.
  - A veto parser: a veto is a reply and the orchestrator queues a fix item. No model-judged style check.
  - q187, q188, q190 (separate queue items); the plugin version (ADR-0039 D4).
- Users will notice: Questions from the pipeline get rarer and read as product decisions; every run and batch report says what users of the product will notice and what was decided without asking; each project needs a one-time upgrade.
- Architecture or behaviour change: yes — the run stops asking about failures, scope, conflicts, secrets and fix wording, and settles them by rule; needs your yes
- Client sign-off: whether a visible change gets a note before building, and whether non-product problems are handled by rule — yes to one short note per plan before building; yes to handling them by rule, with no extra stop when work keeps failing

## Goal

A clean run asks nothing and says what users will notice. A run that meets a product decision asks a question that reads like a colleague explaining it to someone who does not read the code, with a recommendation that is a real judgement. Everything else is settled by a rule and listed in the report, where a reply can still undo it.
